## 2. Data quality findings

**EXP-001 validation: 0 errors, 1 warning, 4 informational. PASS.**

The data is **technically immaculate**: zero nulls across 27 columns and 13,120
rows, zero orphan foreign keys in any direction, zero duplicate keys, zero
duplicate rows, no out-of-domain values, no negative amounts, no unparseable
dates. Every user, course and teacher appears in the transaction table.

**No cleaning was required, and none was performed.** `data/raw/` is byte-identical
before and after.

### Issue register

| # | Severity | Finding | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **I-1** | info | **`Amount` = `CoursePrice` on all 10,000 rows** | "Average spending" is a deterministic function of catalogue choice, not independent behaviour. Answers Q-1 |
| **I-2** | info | **`CourseType` determined by `CoursePrice`** (Free ⟺ 0.00) | The two columns share one bit; using both double-counts |

| **I-4** | warning | Two course names each appear twice | They are **distinct courses** in different categories — not duplicates. `CourseName` cannot identify a course. Answers Q-2 |

One borderline value was examined and **deliberately left alone**: `CR00028` is
typed *Paid* at a price of 0.78. It is internally consistent (Paid ⟺ price > 0);

## 4. EDA conclusions — the decisive finding

### Course choice is indistinguishable from chance

Each statistic compared against a permutation null in which each learner's courses
are redrawn from the empirical popularity distribution, history length held fixed
(200 replicates, seed 42):

| Statistic | Observed | Null mean | Null 95% CI | z | Verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Mean distinct categories | 2.5773 | 2.5869 | [2.571, 2.602] | −1.17 | no signal |
| Mean top-category share | 0.7210 | 0.7194 | [0.717, 0.722] | +1.12 | no signal |
| Mean distinct levels | 1.5560 | 1.5707 | [1.560, 1.582] | −2.65 | marginal |
| Mean free-course share | 0.6501 | 0.6394 | [0.626, 0.654] | +1.51 | no signal |

Restricting to the 1,380 multi-interaction learners gives the same answer. The one
marginal result is **0.03 levels out of 2.2** — at the Bonferroni boundary across
eight tests and negligible in magnitude.

**Item–item co-occurrence is also flat**: observed standard deviation 5.46 against
a null of 5.87 [5.58, 6.12] — *below* the null. There is no collaborative structure.

**Course popularity is near-uniform**: 140–196 enrollments, Gini 0.042, χ² vs
uniform **p = 0.60**.

**Demographics are unrelated to choice**: Gender × Category p = 0.643, Gender ×
Level p = 0.906, AgeBand × Category p = 0.459, AgeBand × Level p = 0.208.

**There is no level progression**: within-learner level slope +0.0053, **p = 0.664**
across 735 eligible learners.

### One real signal: instructor loyalty

| Measure | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Observed distinct teachers per interaction (learners ≥2) | **0.688** |
| Null (teacher drawn at random from those attached to each course) | **0.944** [0.939, 0.950] |
| Heavy cohort: ~13.4 courses from | **2.0 instructors** |
| `Teacher.Expertise` = `Course.Category` | **41.1%** vs 8.3% chance |

**But it predicts the next course only weakly.** Leave-last-out over 1,380
learners: the held-out course falls in the "taught by one of my prior instructors"
pool **49.0%** of the time against a **44.7%** chance rate — a lift of **1.10×**.
Each teacher covers ~15 courses, so knowing two teachers spans nearly half the
catalogue. The signal is real but low-resolution.

### Assessment: the dataset is almost certainly synthetic

Empty band at 5–8 · exactly 5 courses per category · zero nulls across 13,120 rows
· zero orphans in any direction · uniform popularity (p = 0.60), age (p = 0.67),
gender (p = 0.47), payment method (p = 0.40) and daily volume (p = 0.18) ·
sequential gapless IDs · `Amount` ≡ `CoursePrice` with no discount or refund in a
full year.

Instructively, **`Teachers.Expertise` is *not* uniform** and teacher reuse is
strongly non-random — the teacher dimension was modelled with structure, the
course-choice dimension without it.

**This does not invalidate the project.** The methodology, pipeline, leakage
controls and application all transfer unchanged to real data. It constrains the
*claims*: segments describe a generative process, not learner psychology, and
recommendation metrics will be low because the data is nearly random — not because
the methods failed.

### The correlation problem — Phase 1's prediction confirmed

`total_courses` correlates at |r| ≥ 0.8 with **seven** other candidates:

| Pair | r |
| --- | --- |
| `total_courses` ~ `diversity_score` | **+0.983** |
| `avg_courses_per_category` ~ `diversity_ratio` | **−0.984** |
| `total_courses` ~ `teacher_loyalty` | **+0.963** |
| `total_courses` ~ `category_entropy` | +0.924 |

The feature space is **dominated by activity volume**. A K-Means run on the raw
mandated features will largely recover the light/heavy cohort split. The only
candidates carrying non-volume structure are `avg_course_rating`,
`learning_depth_index`, `free_ratio` and the `cat_share_*` vector.

This makes EXP-011e (correlated-feature ablation) and EXP-011f (history-length
dominance) the two most important segmentation experiments, not optional extras.

## 6. Teacher-sheet findings

**EXP-005 refuted the Phase 1 hypothesis.** Q-9 asked whether `TeacherID` was a
deterministic function of `CourseID` — which would have made every teacher feature
an alias and cancelled EXP-014.

| Measure | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Distinct `(course, teacher)` pairs | **887**, not 60 |
| Teachers per course | 7–30 (mean 14.8) |
| Courses per teacher | 7–55 (mean 14.8) |
| Bijection? | **No** |

**EXP-014 proceeds.** Pre-registered expectation **P-6** is half-refuted: the
cancellation branch is closed, and because the teacher dimension holds the only
detectable behavioural signal in the dataset, the "adds nothing" half is now
genuinely uncertain.

| Attribute | Usable | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `TeacherID`, `Expertise`, `YearsOfExperience`, `TeacherRating` | **Yes** | Carry the reuse signal and a course-independent quality prior |
| `TeacherName` | No | PII (§17) |
| `Age`, `Gender` | **No — excluded before testing** | Third-party demographics; routing recommendations by them would be discriminatory allocation (D-016) |

Aggregation to learner level is clean: `n_teachers`, `teacher_loyalty`,
`avg_teacher_rating`. **Redundancy risk is real**: `teacher_loyalty` correlates
+0.963 with `total_courses`, so any apparent segmentation value must be checked
against the volume confound. Teacher features remain **out of the core model** and
enter Phase 3 only as the EXP-014 ablation (§11).

## 7. Leakage risks

| Control | Status |
| --- | --- |
| **L1** — features from the given frame only | **Implemented + tested** |
| **L2** — already-enrolled exclusion from training history | Specified; Phase 3 |
| **L3** — tier assignment from training history only | Specified; Phase 3. **Critical**: 1 train + 1 test interaction must route as tier-1 |
| **L4** — clustering fitted on training features only | Specified; Phase 3 |
| **L5** — popularity from training interactions | **Implemented + tested** |
| **L6** — test interactions in candidate pools | Specified; Phase 3 |

Two further risks surfaced and were handled:

- **Reference-date leakage.** `recency_days` and `first_interaction_days` default
  their reference to the maximum date *within the passed frame*, never the real
  present. Tested.
- **Split drift.** The split is computed once and persisted to
  `data/processed/splits/`; no experiment re-derives it, so tie-breaking
  differences cannot silently make results non-comparable.

## 11. Stop

Per CLAUDE.md §28 and the Phase 2 brief, work **stops here**. Phase 3 will not
begin automatically.

**Phase 3 will, on instruction, run** the 14 segmentation experiments (including
the gap statistic, which can report that no real cluster structure exists) and the
9 recommendation experiments against the persisted Protocol A split — with the
random baseline in every table, coverage as a co-primary metric, and the
pre-registered selection rules applied as written.

Given this audit, the most likely honest outcome is that **no recommender
meaningfully beats random on this dataset**. The evaluation design built in Phase 1
exists precisely so that outcome can be reported clearly rather than concealed.
